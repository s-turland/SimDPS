Abstract

We propose a music inpainting technique combining similarity matching and a diffusion-based generative model to replace large segments of audio recordings with suitable alternatives. The proposed hybrid method, Similarity-Guided Diffusion Posterior Sampling (SimDPS), retrieves candidate replacement segments from the wider signal based on contextual similarity and uses them to guide the diffusion process toward coherent reconstructions. Being model-agnostic, the framework is compatible with any pre-trained diffusion model operating in either the waveform or latent domain. Objective evaluations demonstrate that SimDPS outperforms widely adopted RePaint and DPS methods across acoustic features and neural-embedding distance metrics. Subjective evaluations on multi-genre music datasets confirm that SimDPS enhances perceptual plausibility compared to previous diffusion-based methods and demonstrates robustness to poorly matched similarity candidates. These findings pave the way for improved music restoration and context-aware audio editing.

Listening Test — Diffusion-based Latent Inpainting on General Music

Six excerpts of varying genre were taken from the MUSDB18-HQ test split and assessed by 23 listeners in a multi-stimulus test using webMUSHRA. The masked excerpt was given as the reference for the surrounding musical context, and listeners rated how plausible each reconstruction sounded within that context. Listening Test — Diffusion-based Audio Inpainting on Piano

The protocol was repeated with a waveform-domain prior, an MR-CQTdiff model trained on MAESTRO at 44.1 kHz. Each 6-s piano excerpt contains a 2-s central gap, and Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) serves as the low anchor. SimDPS-Strict and SimDPS-Relaxed differ in their guidance strength. ωX̃, 0.15 and 0.04 respectively.
